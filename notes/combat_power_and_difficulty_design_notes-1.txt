Combat Power & Difficulty — Current Design Notes

1. Core Philosophy

Combat Power (CP) is a balancing tool, not the primary difficulty system.

Floor progression is the main source of difficulty.

Floors naturally become harder as the party progresses.

CP should only provide a gentle adjustment around the expected difficulty of the current run.

CP should represent pure/raw combat power, rather than general usefulness or utility.


2. What Combat Power Represents

CP measures how much direct combat strength a player's build provides.

It does not attempt to measure:

How useful a class is to the team.

How good a player is.

How much utility an ability provides unless that ability directly translates into measurable combat power.


For example:

A Warrior may naturally have high CP because they have high damage/defensive stats.

A Support class may have lower personal CP.

The Support can still make the Warrior dramatically stronger through buffs.


Therefore, individual CP and actual party effectiveness are not necessarily the same thing.

3. CP Calculation

CP should be calculated mathematically from an item's/upgrades' actual stats.

Example:

Sword: 25 damage → 100 CP

Sword upgraded to 30 damage → CP increases accordingly


The important principle is:

> CP comes from stats rather than arbitrary item/rareness values.



This means upgrading an item naturally changes its CP without requiring a separate CP value to be manually assigned to every upgrade.

The exact stat-to-CP conversion weights still need to be defined.

Special abilities/effects may require their own carefully balanced CP contribution if they provide meaningful direct combat power.

4. Persistent Gear vs Run Gear

There are two types of equipment:

Persistent gear

Gear the player owns permanently and chooses to bring into a dungeon.

Run-exclusive gear

Gear obtained during the dungeon run that does not persist after death/end of run.

Both can contribute to the player's current CP.

If a player replaces their persistent weapon with a stronger run-exclusive weapon, their current CP should reflect the gear they're actually able to use.

However, the dungeon difficulty does not continuously react to these changes.

5. Weapons Are Special

Weapons should not simply use the currently equipped weapon for CP.

Players can switch weapons frequently during combat.

For example:

> Scythe → Sword → Scythe → Sword



If CP used the currently held weapon, the player's CP would constantly fluctuate.

Instead:

> Weapon CP = the CP of the strongest usable weapon available to the player during the run.



So if the player has:

Scythe: 100 CP

Sword: 150 CP


Their weapon contribution is 150 CP, regardless of which weapon they currently have in their hand.

This prevents rapid CP fluctuations caused by normal weapon swapping.

Only weapons that are actually part of the player's usable run loadout should count—not arbitrary weapons that are merely stored elsewhere and aren't available to them.

6. Other Equipment

Unlike weapons, things such as:

Armour

Accessories

Passives

Other persistent/run upgrades

Active temporary bonuses where applicable


can contribute normally according to their relevant stats/effects.

The exact treatment of each category still needs to be formally defined.

7. Current CP vs Difficulty CP

An important distinction was established:

Current Combat Power

This represents the player's actual current build.

It can change during a run when they obtain or lose equipment/upgrades.

Example:

> Player starts at 500 CP → finds a powerful run item → now has 550 CP.



The displayed CP can therefore update.

Run Difficulty Modifier

This is not dynamic.

The dungeon calculates the party's CP once, at the start of the run, and uses that to determine the difficulty adjustment.

Once calculated:

> The difficulty modifier is locked for the entire run.



It does not recalculate when:

A new floor is generated.

The player enters a new floor.

A player gets stronger run-exclusive gear.

A player loses gear.

A player switches weapons.

Temporary upgrades change their current CP.


This prevents the dungeon from constantly adapting to player progression.

8. Floor Progression

Floor progression remains independent and is the primary difficulty curve.

For example:

> Floor 1 → Floor 2 → Floor 3 → ... → Floor 10



The dungeon naturally becomes harder through its floor design.

CP simply determines an initial adjustment to that expected difficulty.

Therefore:

> Floor difficulty = primary difficulty



> CP scaling = initial modifier



9. Guaranteed Upgrade Rooms

The guaranteed upgrade rooms, such as the rooms that appear every five floors, are not affected by CP.

They exist according to the dungeon's normal progression rules.

CP should not:

Remove them.

Change their frequency.

Make them stronger/weaker.

Override their guaranteed nature.


There is no meaningful correlation between those progression rules and CP, so they remain independent.

10. Multiplayer CP

Party difficulty uses the players' CP with weighted contributions rather than treating every player's CP equally.

The weakest player contributes 100% of their CP.

The next weakest contributes approximately 80%.

Further players continue with progressively lower weighting.

Conceptually:

> Weakest: 100%
2nd weakest: 80%
3rd weakest: 60%
4th weakest: 40%
etc.



The exact weighting curve/number of players still needs to be finalised.

The purpose is to prevent one extremely overpowered player from disproportionately determining the dungeon's difficulty while still allowing their strength to contribute.

11. CP UI

The player's CP should be directly visible.

Item Lore

Combat Power should also be displayed in the lore of the items that possess combat power.

Each item that contributes CP shows its own CP value on its lore, so a player can see that item's contribution at a glance without opening the breakdown GUI.

The existing player nametag already displays information such as:

> Player Name
Health
Mana
Shield
etc.



CP should simply become another row:

> Combat Power: 523



There is no need for a complicated primary CP interface.

Detailed CP Breakdown

A separate chest-style GUI can be opened to explain where the player's CP comes from.

For example:

> Total Combat Power: 523

Weapon — 180
Armour — 120
Accessories — 73
Passives — 100
Other — 50



This allows players to see the mathematical source of their CP while keeping the normal HUD simple.

12. Run-Start Metadata Sync

Because Combat Power is displayed in item lore, every Dung item that carries a CP value must
stay in sync with the player's actual build.

Some items may exist without a CP value — for example gear obtained or stored before the CP
system was introduced, or items that were created without a recorded CP.

To keep the lore accurate, each run start should include a check/refresh step:

> On run start, check every Dung item the player has (inventory, armour, offhand) for a missing
> or stale Combat Power value, and add/update that Combat Power on the item accordingly.



This ensures that:

Items acquired before the CP system display their CP once the next run begins.

Items whose stats changed (e.g. upgrades, reforges, salvage) get their CP lore updated even if
that change happened outside a run.

The lore CP and the player's computed CP never drift apart.

The displayed lore CP always reflects the item's actual combat contribution at the time of the
run, even though the run's difficulty modifier itself stays locked for the whole run (see
section 7 — the two are independent).

Like the difficulty modifier, this sync runs at run start rather than continuously reacting to
in-run changes; it is a one-time reconciliation of item lore with the CP model.

13. What Is Still Undecided

All items below are now resolved. Sections 14 (stat → CP conversion) and 15 (difficulty
modifier) give the concrete numbers; this section records each decision in place.

Things still needing concrete values/rules:

1. Stat → CP conversion

DECIDED (baseline weights) — see section 14. 1 damage = 4 CP, 1 magic damage = 4 CP, 1 defense =
2 CP, 1 health = 0.5 CP, 1 shield capacity = 1 CP, 1 reach = 5 CP, affix value = 1.0/vpt, crit
folded into each item from its rarity (Option A). (Attack speed is not an item stat and carries
no CP.) If live play later suggests different numbers, they change as tuning — not undecided.


Resolved decisions:

2. Special effects

DECIDED — special effects stay untouched. Effects that don't directly correspond to a simple
stat are not valued for CP and are left as-is; they do not receive a CP contribution from the
special-effects branch.

3. Support abilities

DECIDED — support abilities stay untouched. Direct buffs do not contribute any CP at all;
CP intentionally represents individual raw power rather than team utility, so a Support class's
buffs are not counted toward its own CP.

4. Exact multiplayer weighting

DECIDED — weighting as discussed: least-powerful player contributes 100% of their CP, the next
80%, then 60%, then 40%, and so on. This weighted-party CP is used for the run's difficulty
modifier. (The exact continuation beyond 4 players, if ever needed, can still be confirmed.)

5. Exact difficulty modifier

DECIDED (structural + locked-in defaults) — see section 15. modifier = clamp((weightedPartyCP /
referenceCP(startFloor) - 1.0) * 1.0, ±10% early, ±20% late), computed once at run start and
locked; 75% applied to encounter complexity, 25% to enemy HP/damage. The caps keep CP from
overriding floor progression.

6. Temporary effects counted toward displayed current CP

DECIDED — as discussed. Current CP reflects the gear the player is actually able to use,
including run-exclusive gear and applicable active temporary bonuses, while the dungeon's
CP-based difficulty modifier stays locked at run start (section 7) and is not affected by these
temporary changes.



Core rule in one sentence

> Combat Power is a mathematically calculated measurement of a player's raw combat strength; it can change as their build changes, but the party's CP-based dungeon difficulty adjustment is calculated once at run start and remains fixed, while floor progression remains the primary source of difficulty.


14. Stat → CP Conversion (Plan)

CP must be derived from actual item/upgrade stats (section 3 — "CP comes from stats rather
than arbitrary item/rareness values"). The plan below gives every stat a per-unit CP weight so
upgrading an item naturally changes its CP with no per-upgrade manual values.

What we can actually read off an item (from the codebase):

- Weapon: DAMAGE (melee) or MAGIC_DAMAGE (magic weapons), optional HEALTH affix, optional REACH,
  optional ABILITY/COST, plus UPGRADE_LEVEL and AFFIXES.
- Armor: DEFENSE, optional HEALTH, plus UPGRADE_LEVEL and AFFIXES.
- Shield: SHIELD_MAX (capacity), plus UPGRADE_LEVEL and AFFIXES.
- Rarity (RARITY tag) — used only to derive the crit chance / crit multiplier bonus the item
  grants (those are computed from rarity in PlayerState, not stored as item tags).

NOT computable from item data (so deliberately absent from per-item CP):

- Attack speed / fire rate — this is a FIXED per-class value (default 7 ticks, 5 for Rangers),
  never derived from any gear stat. Items do not change swing rate, so an item cannot have
  "DPS" or attack-speed CP.
- Overall DPS / "max DPS" — depends on the class's fixed fire rate plus the weapon's damage and
  crit; it is a build-level figure, not an item property. Any DPS-language in this plan refers
  to comparing weapon damage, not a per-item attack-rate stat.
- Move speed / max mana — only reached on the combined PlayerState, not on individual items
  (see player-total section below). (Crit is NOT in this list — under Option A it is folded into
  each item's CP from its rarity.)

Guiding principle:

> Every stat's CP contribution is proportional to how much direct combat strength it gives,
> expressed in a common "damage-equivalent" unit. Weights are designed so a typical weapon and
> a typical armor piece land in the same broad CP band, and so higher tiers scale up naturally.

The nominal weights (tunable, but this is the starting cadence):

| Stat | Source | Weight (CP per unit) | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Weapon damage | DAMAGE tag | 4.0 | Core damage-dealer stat |
| Magic damage | MAGIC_DAMAGE tag | 4.0 | Matched to melee damage per point |
| Defense | DEFENSE tag | 2.0 | Reduces incoming damage |
| Health | HEALTH affix | 0.5 | Adds survivability, less direct than damage |
| Shield capacity | SHIELD_MAX tag | 1.0 | Adds an absorb pool |
| Reach | REACH tag | 5.0 | Adds melee range/utility |
| Affix value | each affix roll value | 1.0 per value point | Affixes are stored separately (AFFIXES tag) and NOT written back into the base stat tag, so base-stat CP + affix-value CP do not double count |

Upgrade level is NOT a separate CP weight:

- The UPGRADE workstation raises an item's core stat by +10%/level
  (WorkstationRules.upgradeStatMult = 1.0 + 0.10 * level) and WRITES that boosted value back
  into the item's own stat tag (GearFactory.setUpgradeLevel).
- So the base-stat weight already captures the upgrade's strength. Adding a separate
  "8.0 per upgrade level" line would double-count the same boosted damage/defense/etc.
- The UPGRADE_LEVEL tag is still useful for DISPLAY ("Upgrade V") and to know the multiplier,
  but it contributes no additional CP of its own.

Crit is folded into each item's CP (Option A — DECIDED):

- critChance and critMult are derived from RARITY at recompute time, not stored as item tags.
- Effective-damage math: crit chance c and crit mult m raise average damage by factor
  1 + c(m-1).
- DECIDED: Option A — each item's CP includes its own rarity-derived crit contribution, applied
  to that item's own damage (for weapons) or armor-set damage context. So an item's lore CP =
  direct stats + its rarity crit, and the breakdown GUI shows per-item numbers that already
  include crit.
- Because crit is rarity-derived, it rises naturally with tier (a Mythic weapon carries more
  crit CP than a Common one at the same base damage).

How to compute a total item CP (per item):

- Read the item's stat tags (DAMAGE / MAGIC_DAMAGE / DEFENSE / HEALTH / SHIELD_MAX / REACH),
  plus UPGRADE_LEVEL and AFFIXES.
- Multiply each stat by its weight and sum. This is the item's CP.
- Weapons: use the item's own stat tags; the item's CP is independent of whether it is currently
  held (the player's weapon contribution uses the best usable weapon per section 5).
- Magic weapons: only MAGIC_DAMAGE contributes as a damage stat (their melee damage is always 1
  and should not add CP, since its basic swings are negligible by design).
- Armor: sum defense + health affix + upgrade-boosted stats + affixes, plus the piece's own
  rarity-derived crit CP contribution.
- Shields: shield capacity * 1.0 + affixes, plus the shield's rarity-derived crit CP
  contribution.

Player total CP (current build):

- Weapon contribution = CP of the strongest usable weapon (section 5) — the best weapon the
  player can actually use this run counts, NOT only the currently held one.
- Plus ALL equipped armor (all 4 slots), each computed by the same per-item sum. This includes:
  - Each armor piece's DEFENSE (→ CP via the defense weight), and
  - Each armor piece's HEALTH affix (→ CP via the health weight). Health from armor rises
    maxHearts through the reservoir, so it contributes as survivability just like a weapon's
    health affix.
- Plus accessories, shields, and applicable temporary bonuses (section 6), each computed by the
  same per-item sum.
- Plus permanent upgrade-track contributions computed from the same weights:
  - Damage upgrade: +4.0 * level (matches the +1 damage/level).
  - Defense upgrade: +2.0 * level.
  - Crit upgrade: +3.0 per 1% (crit upgrade = +0.5%/level → +1.5 CP/level), folded into each
    upgraded piece's crit CP if applicable.
  - Speed upgrade: +40.0 * 0.03 per level → +1.2 CP/level (mobility, capped).
  - Mana upgrade: +0.15 * 5 per level → +0.75 CP/level.
  - Max hearts upgrade: +0.5 * 5 per level → +2.5 CP/level.
  - Magic damage upgrade: +4.0 * level.

In short, the player's total CP = best-weapon CP + armor CP (defense AND health, incl. each
piece's crit) + accessories + shields + temporary bonuses + permanent upgrades.

Calibration target:

- A COMMON starter weapon (~5 damage) should land near ~20 CP (its small crit contribution
  included).
- A COMMON cloth armor piece (~1 defense) should land near ~2 CP.
- Higher rarities push stats up via statMult AND carry more crit CP, so CP rises naturally with
  tier without any rarity→CP lookup table (rare/epic/mythic items are just items with bigger
  stat values + more crit).

Still-to-confirm (kept as open, see section 13):

- The exact per-1%-crit CP number is locked in at 3.0 CP per 1% (matching the crit upgrade note
  above; crit upgrade = +0.5%/level → +1.5 CP/level).

Decided within this plan:

- Move speed / max mana: KEPT at their small multipliers (retained in the plan, not excluded
  or capped further).

The weights above are a starting baseline, not final numbers. They are centralized as a single
conversion table (the "CP weights") so they can be adjusted in one place once combat math is
validated.


15. Difficulty Modifier (Plan)

This section turns the CP value into the run's difficulty adjustment. It must satisfy the
constraints already locked in by sections 1, 4, 5, and 7:

- Floor progression is the PRIMARY difficulty driver; CP is only a gentle adjustment (section 1).
- The modifier is computed ONCE from the weighted-party CP at run start and LOCKED for the run
  (section 7) — the player's later CP changes never affect it.
- It can only nudge difficulty within a bounded range — exactly ±10% on the early floors
  (floor <= 5), widening to exactly ±20% on the later floors (floor > 5) (original design,
  section 4) — it can never turn Floor 2 into a late-game floor or remove the challenge from a
  deep floor.
- It should prefer ENCOUNTER VARIETY / COMPLEXITY over raw enemy HP and damage inflation
  (original design, section 5): more elites, more dangerous combinations, more mechanics — not
  bigger health bars.

Step 1 — Expected CP baseline per floor.

Because floor depth drives difficulty, define a "reference" CP the game considers normal at a
given floor. This is the CP a correctly-progressed solo player is expected to have when that
floor becomes reachable.

Locked-in reference-CP constants:

> referenceCP(floor) = baseCP_start + (floor - 1) * cpPerFloor
> baseCP_start = 25  (a starter kit at Floor 1)
> cpPerFloor = 12

So referenceCP(1) = 25, referenceCP(5) = 73, referenceCP(10) = 133. These fixed defaults track how
CP actually grows per floor with the weights from section 14.

Step 2 — Compute weighted party CP (locked at run start).

Use the decided weighting (section 13 item 4): sort party members by their intended-run CP
(current build, section 6), weakest first, and apply 100% / 80% / 60% / 40%...

> weightedPartyCP = 100%·weakest + 80%·next + 60%·next + 40%·next (if 4 members)

A solo player is just 100% of their own CP. This value is computed once and never changes
mid-run (section 7).

Step 3 — Normalize to a "CP ratio".

Compare the party against the reference for the FIRST floor they enter (or the starting floor):

> cpRatio = weightedPartyCP / referenceCP(startFloor)

- cpRatio ≈ 1.0 → party is right on the expected curve → modifier ≈ neutral.
- cpRatio > 1.0 → party is stronger than expected → difficulty nudges up.
- cpRatio < 1.0 → party is weaker than expected → difficulty nudges down (never below a floor).

Step 4 — Bound the modifier.

Clamp the raw ratio into the allowed gentle range so CP can't override floor progression:

> modifier = clamp((cpRatio - 1.0) * sensitivity, minCap, maxCap)

- sensitivity = 1.0 — how fast the adjustment grows with a CP mismatch.
- Early floors (floor <= 5): minCap/maxCap = ±0.10 (±10%).
- Later floors (floor > 5): minCap/maxCap = ±0.20 (±20%). The caps step at the floor-5
  threshold.

The caps are the hard guarantee that Floor 2 can't become a late-game floor and a deep floor
can't be trivialized — the floor's own design is always the dominant difficulty.

Step 5 — Apply the modifier.

Prefer complexity, not HP/DPS inflation (original design, section 5):

- Convert `modifier` mostly into ENCOUNTER COMPLEXITY:
  - Elite-chance: higher party CP raises the chance a combat room rolls elite enemies.
  - Combat composition: more advanced/dangerous enemy combinations and additional enemy types
    appearing together.
  - Room variants: strong parties are more likely to get the harder room variants.
  - Mechanics: more frequent enemy/boss mechanics.
- Apply only a SMALL fraction of the modifier directly to enemy HP/damage (the existing
  floor + party-size scaling), so strong parties feel a bit more pressure without sponges.
  The locked-in split: 75% of the modifier goes to complexity, 25% to a bounded HP/damage
  nudge (mirrors the "prefer variety over inflation" principle).

Rebalancing the HP lever that already exists:

- Enemy HP already scales with floor and party size (MobType.hpAt: baseHp·(1+floor·0.5), with a
  party-size hpMult 1+0.3·(partySize-1)). The modifier ADDS a small, capped multiplier on top of
  this — it does not replace floor or party scaling.

Step 6 — Lock and persist.

- Compute `weightedPartyCP` and `modifier` once when the run starts (DungeonInstance.startRun),
  store them on the instance, and reuse for every floor of that run.
- Do NOT recompute on floor generation, descend, gear gain/loss, weapon switch, or CP change
  (section 7). The modifier is fixed.

Why this satisfies the philosophy:

- Strong parties get harder-but-fair encounters (complexity) instead of damage sponges, and can
  still clear early floors fast (section 1/7 of the original doc — veterans being faster is fine).
- Weak/mixed parties aren't punished: the weighting favors the weakest members, and the caps keep
  the adjustment gentle, so a new player grouped with a veteran faces a fairer floor.
- The modifier is a static, per-run number: no constant re-scaling, no CP-powered difficulty
  whiplash, floor progression stays the main curve.

Locked-in default values (concrete, not left to tune later):

- Reference-CP curve: baseCP_start = 25, cpPerFloor = 12.
- Sensitivity = 1.0.
- Caps: exactly ±0.10 (±10%) on the early floors (floor <= 5), widening to exactly ±0.20 (±20%)
  on the late floors (floor > 5). Caps step at the floor-5 threshold rather than sliding smoothly.
- Complexity-vs-HP split: exactly 75% of the modifier goes to encounter complexity
  (elite chance, combos, room variants, mechanics), 25% to a bounded enemy HP/damage nudge.

These are fixed defaults. If live play later shows they need adjustment, they change like any
tuning decision — but they are not left undecided here.
